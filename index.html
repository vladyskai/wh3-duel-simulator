How the answer is computed

1. One swing is a weighted coin

Every swing hits or misses. Only the gap between the attacker's attack and the defender's defence matters.

Formula

A probability cannot leave the range 0 to 1, hence the min and max. The game itself clamps to 8% to 90% (not applied here).

2. Hits needed is a fixed number

Hit points and damage only matter through this count. Each block is one hit.

Formula

The brackets mean "round up": 120 HP at 25 damage needs 5 hits, not 4.8.

3. Swings needed is random

Count the swings until the k-th hit. That count follows the negative binomial distribution. Dashed lines mark the means.

Formula

The last swing must be a hit (one factor p). The n−1 swings before it hold exactly k−1 hits in any order (the binomial coefficient).

4. Swings become a time

Both soldiers swing first at t = 0, then once per attack interval. A slower soldier's curve is stretched to the right.

Formula

a and b are the two attack intervals. Swing 1 lands at t = 0, swing n at t = a(n−1).

5. The race

For each moment A could land its killing blow, take the chance that B has not finished yet. Add them up.

Formula

m(n) is how many swings B has made by the time A's n-th swing lands.

Both die only when B swings at exactly the same instant and that swing is B's killing blow.

6. How long the fight lasts

The fight ends at the first killing blow. It is still going at time t only if both soldiers are still standing.

Formula

The two soldiers' luck is independent, so the chances multiply.
