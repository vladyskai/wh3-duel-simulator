How the answer is computed

1. One swing is a weighted coin

Every swing hits or misses. Only the gap between the attacker's attack and the defender's defence matters.

Formula

A probability cannot leave the range 0 to 1, hence the min and max. The game itself clamps to 8% to 90% (not applied here yet).

2. Damage per hit is random

Armour-piercing damage always lands in full. Armour blocks a random share of the base damage on every hit. The solid part is guaranteed, the striped part is the armour roll, the tick is the average.

Formula

U is the armour roll: each hit, armour blocks between half and all of its value, as a percentage of the base damage. Above 100 armour the block is capped at 100%, so some hits lose their whole base damage. This rule is how the player community describes the game. It has not been checked against the engine here.

3. Hits needed is now random too

Keep adding random hits until they reach the enemy's hit points. The count of hits is no longer a fixed number but a distribution.

Formula

The sum of k random hits has its own distribution: the convolution of k copies of one hit's distribution. The page computes it numerically, one hit at a time. With no armour every hit is the same and this falls back to the old rule, k = HP / D rounded up.

4. Swings needed: a mixture

For each possible number of hits, the swings follow a negative binomial, as before. The real distribution blends them, weighted by how likely each number of hits is. Dashed lines mark the means.

Formula

The variance splits in two (the law of total variance): the luck of hitting or missing, plus the luck of the armour rolls. The hit-or-miss coin and the armour roll are independent.

5. Swings become a time

Both soldiers swing first at t = 0, then once per attack interval. A slower soldier's curve is stretched to the right.

Formula

a and b are the two attack intervals. Swing 1 lands at t = 0, swing n at t = a(n−1).

6. The race

For each moment A could land its killing blow, take the chance that B has not finished yet. Add them up.

Formula

m(n) is how many swings B has made by the time A's n-th swing lands.

Both die only when B swings at exactly the same instant and that swing is B's killing blow.

7. How long the fight lasts

The fight ends at the first killing blow. It is still going at time t only if both soldiers are still standing.

Formula

The two soldiers' luck is independent, so the chances multiply.
